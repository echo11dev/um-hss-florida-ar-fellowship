Mission

The University of Miami/Jackson Memorial Orthopedic Adult Reconstructive Surgery Fellowship Program is committed to the education of fellows and to preparing these physicians to function as well-qualified, independent specialists. The faculty’s role is to instill the traits essential to success, including honesty and integrity, objectivity, self-motivation, curiosity, timeliness, and a sense of responsibility. The division is committed to a culture of diversity, equity, and inclusion, because varied perspectives and backgrounds improve the faculty and the care of patients.

To achieve our mission, we are committed to train our fellows to understand the process of quality research and to critically evaluate literature. To graduate surgeons who are capable of practicing competently and independently. To train fellows to master all aspects of adult reconstruction surgery, including primary, revision, periprosthetic joint infection, and periprosthetic fractures. To graduate surgeons who are competitive applicants for positions in either private practice or academic practice settings. To graduate surgeons who perform well in subspecialty practice. To recruit superior fellowship candidates.   • Year 1

    The fellowship is a one-year clinical training program. The year is dedicated to adult reconstruction surgery of the hip and knee. Published rotations are at University of Miami Hospital and Jackson Memorial Hospital. The division operates five days a week. Cases include primary and revision arthroplasty and the pathologies seen in a referral practice: arthritis, avascular necrosis, dysplasia, infection, and periprosthetic fracture. Fellows typically operate four days a week and spend one day in clinic.

    The fellow teaches residents in clinic and in the operating room. Orthopaedic surgery residents rotate on the adult reconstruction service as a PGY-2, PGY-4, and PGY-5. Medical students rotate on the service as well.

    Department faculty also practice in the UHealth–Hospital for Special Surgery musculoskeletal program at UHealth SoLé Mia in North Miami. That collaboration is described on the history page. The Miller School and Jackson Health System pages list University of Miami Hospital and Jackson Memorial Hospital as the fellowship rotations.
